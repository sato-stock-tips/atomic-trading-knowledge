**William O'Neil's Explanation of a Follow-Through Day (FTD)**

In William J. O'Neil's CAN SLIM methodology — the core framework from *How to Make Money in Stocks* — the **Follow-Through Day** serves as the critical confirmation signal that a **market correction** or **bear phase** has likely ended and a new **confirmed uptrend** (new bull market leg) [[trending market]] is beginning. O'Neil stresses that no major bull market in history has started without one, though not every FTD succeeds.

### Key Criteria for a Classic Follow-Through Day
O'Neil (and IBD's ongoing application of his rules) looks for the following on one of the major indexes (primarily **Nasdaq Composite** or **S&P 500** - tickers are QQQ or NQ1! or ES1! or SPY or SPX):

- Occurs **Day 4 or later** (most powerful on **Days 4–7**) of an **attempted rally** off a recent low.
  - The rally attempt begins with the first up day after the downtrend (Day 1).
  - The rally attempt stays valid as long as the index avoids making a new low.
- A **significant gain** — historically **≥1%–1.25%** (often 1.7%+ in more recent/more conservative interpretations for stronger conviction).
- **Higher volume** (heavier trading [[volume]]) than the prior session — shows institutional buying/sponsorship (smart money [[institutional accumulation]]).
- The move should feel **explosive** and decisive — not a marginal, barely qualifying gain.

### Why the Follow-Through Day Matters in Trader Terminology
- It confirms **institutional demand** is returning after a period of **distribution** (institutional selling pressure during the prior downtrend/correction).
- It signals the end of the **shakeout** phase and the start of a potential **markup** phase in the broader market cycle.
- Traders use the FTD as the "green light" to become more aggressive: begin increasing exposure, focus on leading stocks breaking out of proper bases (cup-with-handle, double bottom, flat base, etc.), and buy on strength rather than anticipating bottoms.

### Additional Context from O'Neil's Approach
- **Leading stocks** should show strength — many top relative strength names often lead or coincide with the FTD.
- **Failed FTDs** occur when the rally undercuts the **Day 1 low** of the attempted rally (or sometimes when multiple FTDs fail) → this resets the rally count and often signals the bear market/low remains in control.
- O'Neil emphasizes patience: wait for the FTD rather than bottom-fishing prematurely. Premature buying during failed rally attempts leads to whipsaws and drawdowns.
- Once confirmed, watch for **distribution days** (significant down days on higher volume) to gauge when the uptrend may be weakening later.

In short, O'Neil views the Follow-Through Day as the single most important timing tool for shifting from **defensive** (cash-heavy or short-biased during corrections) to **offensive** mode — aggressively pursuing high-quality breakouts in leading growth stocks during the early stages of a new uptrend.
