
A true Follow-Through Day then confirms the shift on Day 4 or later of that attempted rally (most powerful on Days 4–7). It requires two key criteria:

1.  Significant price gain: The Nasdaq or S&P 500 closes up at least 1% (often cited as 1–1.25% or more) from the previous day’s close. Some updated analyses use ~1.7% as a stronger threshold. 

2.  Higher volume: Trading volume must be greater than the previous day’s volume (it doesn’t necessarily have to be above average, just heavier than the day before). 

Why It Matters

•  It confirms institutional buying: The big gain on rising volume signals that “smart money” (large institutions) is stepping back into the market aggressively. 

•  Avoids guessing the bottom: Instead of relying on gut feel, the FTD gives a clear, rules-based signal to start buying leading growth stocks.

•  Historical reliability: O’Neil’s studies found it marks the start of nearly all major bull markets, though not every FTD leads to a sustained rally (some fail if the market later undercuts its recent lows).
