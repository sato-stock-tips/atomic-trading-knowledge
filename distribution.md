# Distribution

**Distribution** is the market phase (Stage 3) where institutional investors systematically sell their positions into strength, transferring shares from strong hands to weak hands. It marks the transition from [[markup]] (uptrend) to markdown (downtrend).

## How to Identify Distribution

### Volume Signal

From [[volume]]:
> "High volume at tops (distribution), low volume at bottoms ([[institutional accumulation]] complete)"

High [[volume]] on down days or price stalling on heavy volume indicates selling pressure from large players.

### Follow Through Day Context

From [[follow through day]]:
> "It confirms institutional demand is returning after a period of distribution (institutional selling pressure during the prior downtrend/correction)."

Distribution precedes corrections and bear markets. A [[follow through day]] signals the end of distribution and return of institutional buying.

### Intraday Signs

From [[exit parabolic stocks]]:
> "Failed Intra-Day Bounce: If a parabolic stock can't reclaim its morning highs, distribution has started."

When [[extended stocks]] fail to hold intraday strength, institutions are likely distributing shares to retail buyers chasing momentum.

## What It Means

Distribution is the opposite of [[institutional accumulation]]:
- **Accumulation**: Institutions buying quietly, absorbing supply
- **Distribution**: Institutions selling into demand, offloading positions

## Stage Analysis Context

Distribution represents **Stage 3** in the Stan Weinstein framework:
- Stage 1: Accumulation (basing)
- Stage 2: [[markup]] (uptrend)
- **Stage 3: Distribution (topping)**
- Stage 4: Markdown (downtrend)

## Trading Implications

When you identify distribution:
1. Avoid new long entries
2. Consider [[sell into strength]] on existing positions
3. Tighten [[stop loss]] levels
4. Look for [[when not to trade]] signals in the broader [[market trend model]]

Distribution is a warning sign to protect capital, not a signal to short (unless you have a clear [[setup]]).
