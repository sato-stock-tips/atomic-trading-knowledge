## **How I Decide When _Not_ to Trade**

_Most traders obsess over entries. I spend more time deciding **when not to trade**. That decision alone saved me more money than any setup ever made me._

### **The 10-Step Framework**

1. [[market trend model]]:** I start with my market trend model. It tells me if the environment supports new long exposure or not. When the signal is **red**, I do not initiate any new trades. Period.
    
2. **Yellow Phases:** Yellow is not “almost green or red.” Yellow means caution. My studies show very clearly that trades initiated during yellow phases often turn into frustration trades. Small wins, quick stop-outs, no follow-through. I stay mostly in cash.
    
3. **Statistical Reality:** This is not opinion. It’s data. Trades taken during red and yellow phases have a much lower success rate in my system. I don’t argue with statistics — I respect them.
    
4. **Sideways Markets:** Sideways markets are dangerous. Trend direction changes every few weeks or months. Rallies fail quickly. Breakouts don’t follow through. This is where most traders slowly bleed capital.
    
5. **False Signals:** My model is trend-following by design. In sideways markets, even good systems give false signals. I accept that limitation — and step aside instead of forcing trades.
    
6. **Choppy Price Action:** When indices grind up and down without progress, and leadership constantly rotates, I know it’s not a market to press exposure. Patience beats activity here.
    
7. **No Setups, No Trades:** I only trade clear, high-quality setups. If my watchlist is empty or filled with “almost” setups, I do nothing. I don’t lower standards to feel productive.
    
8. **Forcing Is the Enemy:** Forcing trades usually comes from boredom or pressure. I refuse to trade just because time passes. The market doesn’t owe me opportunities. [[don't overtrade]]
    
9. **Time Is Not the Constraint:** I am fully okay waiting weeks — even months — for the right conditions. Big money is made during short windows. Everything else is preparation.
    
10. **[[go to cash]] Is a Position:** Sitting in cash is not failure. It’s discipline. It keeps my mind clear, my confidence intact, and my capital ready for the next real opportunity.
