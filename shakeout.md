# Shakeout

A **shakeout** is a deliberate or natural price decline designed to flush out weak hands — traders with poor conviction who sell on fear — before the real move higher begins.

## Nicolas Darvas on Shakeouts

From [[darvas box]]:
> "Within the range of a box, Darvas considered movement to the bottom of the range a healthy sign. These moves down shakeout weak holders who sell thinking the downward trend may continue."
> — Nicolas Darvas, "How I Made $2 Million in the Stock Market"

Shakeouts are **healthy** because they transfer shares from nervous sellers to stronger hands who understand the bigger picture.

## Qullamaggie's Perspective

From [[high tight flag]]:
> "Shakeouts Allowed: Minor undercuts of the MA (e.g., 1-day dips) are fine if bought up quickly, as they flush out weak hands and attract shorts to cover."

Brief violations of [[support & resistance]] levels or [[mark minervini (key moving averages)]] are acceptable **if** price is quickly reclaimed. This violent snap-back traps shorts and clears sellers.

## The Transition Point

From [[follow through day]]:
> "It signals the end of the shakeout phase and the start of a potential [[markup]] phase in the broader market cycle."

At market bottoms, the final shakeout creates the capitulation low. When strong hands absorb all the selling and a [[follow through day]] occurs, [[institutional accumulation]] is complete.

## Within Consolidations

From [[five star setups]]:
> "Tight Consolidation: After the run, price must compress... This is where weak hands get shaken out and strong hands stay in control."

During [[consolidation]], minor shakeouts below key levels (with low [[volume]]) test conviction. The stock should reclaim those levels quickly, forming [[higher lows]] over time.

## How to Identify a Healthy Shakeout

1. **Quick Reversal**: Price undercuts support but closes above it same day
2. **Low Volume**: Selling isn't heavy — just enough to trigger stops
3. **Relative Strength Holds**: Stock remains stronger than the market
4. **[[volume]] Expansion on Recovery**: Institutions step in to absorb supply

## Shakeout vs Real Breakdown

| **Shakeout** | **Real Breakdown** |
|--------------|-------------------|
| Brief undercut | Sustained breakdown |
| Low [[volume]] | Heavy volume |
| Quick recovery | No recovery |
| [[relative strength]] holds | RS collapses |

## Trading Implications

Shakeouts create opportunity for those with conviction:
- Use them as entry points if your [[setup]] criteria are met
- Don't panic sell during minor violations
- Watch for the bounce with [[volume]] confirmation
- Distinguish healthy shakeouts from actual trend failures

The best traders understand that shakeouts are features, not bugs — they're necessary to build energy for the next leg higher.
