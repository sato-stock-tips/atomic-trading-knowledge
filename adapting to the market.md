**Adapting to the Market: Stay in Sync or Pay the Price**

Adapting to the market is one of the most important skills I’ve learned as a trader. The market changes its character constantly, and my job is not to predict it, but to stay in sync with it. When I’m aligned, trading feels easier. When I’m not, losses show up fast.

1. **Portfolio Feedback:** My portfolio is my honest mirror. If my equity curve is rising, I’m likely in sync with the market environment. If it’s grinding down, something is off. I don’t argue with that signal. I listen. The portfolio tells me early if my setups fit the current market.
    
2. **Daily Portfolio Review:** I check how my open positions behave relative to the market. Are breakouts following through? Are strong stocks holding gains? If even my best setups fail, that’s not bad luck — it’s market information.
    
3. **Screeners as a Market Thermometer:** My screeners show me what the market rewards right now. I watch how many stocks qualify and, more importantly, which types of stocks show up with [[relative strength]]. Growth, defensive, cyclicals, commodities? A shrinking list is a warning sign.
    
4. **Watchlist Quality Over Quantity:** I don’t care about having a long watchlist. I care about how many clean, actionable setups I see. When the watchlist dries up or fills with sloppy charts, I slow down automatically. That’s the market telling me to be patient.
    
5. **[[industry (previously known as sectors)]] and Theme Rotation:** I always ask: where is money flowing _now_? Not last quarter. Not last year. If leadership shifts, I shift with it. Fighting rotation is one of the fastest ways to lose sync with the market.
    
6. **[[market trend model]]:** My trend model gives me structure. Is the market in an uptrend, downtrend, or sideways phase? Are there early warning signs of a correction? I don’t need perfection — I need direction. My exposure follows the model, not my opinion.
    
7. **Index Price Action:** I watch how the major indices move, not just where they close. Are rallies smooth or choppy? Are sell-offs fast or controlled? Price action tells me if institutions are confident or defensive.
    
8. **Combining All Inputs:** No single signal decides my actions. I combine portfolio feedback, screeners, the [[when not to trade]], and index behavior to form one clear picture. That picture tells me what kind of market we are in — and what kind of trader I need to be right now.
    
9. **Daily Homework Is Non-Negotiable:** I show up every day. Skipping preparation is dangerous. If you disappear for weeks and then jump back in, you usually re-enter at the worst possible time — already holding stocks that are breaking down.
    
10. **Sideways Markets Demand Precision:** Sideways markets change direction fast. One week momentum works, the next it doesn’t. This is where staying in sync matters most. I trade smaller, I’m more selective, and I accept that cash is often the best position.
    
11. **Let the Market Lead:** I don’t force trades. I let the market invite me in. When conditions improve, setups appear naturally. When they disappear, I step back. That’s how I stay aligned — and that’s how I survive long enough to thrive.
