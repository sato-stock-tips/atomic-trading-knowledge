How to Spot True Institutional Accumulation

Institutions leave footprints in the chart — you just need to know where to look. True accumulation is what separates random moves from real growth potential. Here’s how to spot when big money is building a position and how to act on it.

### Chart Annotations

- **Above average volume!**
    
- **Big rally!**
    
- **Volume dry up**
    
- **Clear base**
    
- **Volume picks up again**
    

---

1. **Repeated Up-Days on [[volume]]** Watch for several days where the stock trades +1.5–2x average volume while closing strong. This is a sign funds are quietly loading up.
    
2. **Quiet Pullbacks:** Healthy accumulation shows in red days that are light in volume. Weak selling pressure signals demand is outweighing supply.
    
3. **Tight Trading Ranges:** A stock that holds in a narrow range after a big move tells you institutions are absorbing supply without letting it break down. e.g. [[inside day]]
    
4. **[[higher lows]]:** Each dip is bought up faster, creating a staircase pattern. This is accumulation pressure, not random noise.
    
5. **Strong [[relative strength]] Line:** While the general market chops, the RS line should make new highs. Funds don't waste time with laggards — they back leaders.
    
6. **Support at [[mark minervini (key moving averages)]]:** Accumulated stocks often respect EMA 10 or EMA 20. Each test brings in new buying interest instead of breaking lower.
    
7. **Volume Dry-Ups at Lows:** When a stock pulls back into support and volume collapses, it often signals sellers are done — setting up the next accumulation phase.
    
8. **Cluster of Breakouts in a [[Sector]]:** Institutions rarely buy one stock alone. If multiple names in the same theme show accumulation, the money flow is real.
    
9. **Earnings as a Catalyst:** Accumulation often intensifies around earnings. Look for [[gaps]] that hold and volume spikes on results — that's conviction buying.
    
10. **No Panic on Market Weakness:** The best tells? A stock that refuses to drop even when the indexes are red. That's institutional sponsorship at work.
