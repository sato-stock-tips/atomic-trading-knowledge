**The Boredom Trap: Why Trading for Action Destroys Accounts** _Most traders don't lose because they lack knowledge. They lose because they can't sit still._ _When the market feels slow, boredom sets in. And boredom is dangerous. It pushes you into mediocre setups, random trades, and action for the sake of action. That's how accounts bleed slowly. The pros know: patience is part of the job._

**Chart Annotations:**

- Wow! This stock must go to $100!! Let's get in...
    
- Oh! 40% gap down... It will come back
    
- This is the low! I can't sell it now... lost too much money.
    

**1. Impulse Trades:** You see a half-clean base or random news pop and click buy. It feels like "doing something," but the odds are stacked against you. 

**2. No-Setup Entries:** You break your own rules. Instead of a clear [[setups]], you take a messy chart because it's "moving." That's gambling, not trading. 

**3. Chasing Volatility:** When nothing else works, traders jump on the hottest mover of the day — often extended, illiquid, or without structure. This usually ends in whipsaw losses. 

**4. False Progress:** Every impulse trade feels like progress because you're active. But your equity curve doesn't lie. More trades ≠ more gains. 

**5. Mental Fatigue:** Overtrading isn't just financial damage. It drains focus and discipline — so when the real A+ setup comes, you're too tired or hesitant to trade it right. 

**6. Broken Confidence:** After enough boredom trades, you stop trusting yourself. You hesitate even when the setup is right, because your recent history is filled with mistakes. 

**7. Lost Edge:** Trading is about repeating your process. Every time you trade outside of it, you weaken your edge. Small cracks become big leaks.

8. Follow the [[two trades a day per market]] rule to limit FOMO
