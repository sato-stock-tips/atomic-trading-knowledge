
Drawdowns kill confidence, destroy momentum, and push you into emotional trading. Your job isn’t to avoid losses — your job is to keep every drawdown small, controlled, and easy to recover from. These are the ten rules I use to keep my equity curve close to the highs and avoid the deep holes that take months to climb out of.

1. **Risk Per Trade:** I never risk more than 1% of my equity on a single position. Small losses are easy to handle, easy to recover from, and never damage the long-term picture. One mistake should never ruin an entire month. [[R-multiples]]
    
2. **Daily Exposure Cuts:** If my equity drops more than 4% in a single day, I analyze and maybe reduce exposure and frequency. I close weak positions and trim small winners, and stop adding new trades. Big down days are a warning — not something you trade through.
    
3. **Track Your Equity Curve:** Your equity curve is its own system. I monitor it every day and know exactly when I drift into a mistake-phase. Without tracking, you will walk blind into large drawdowns.
    
4. **Use Moving Averages on Equity:** I use a 20-day MA and Bollinger Bands on my equity curve. When equity closes below the MA20, I reduce trading frequency and become extremely selective. This rule alone prevents the majority of emotional damage.
    
5. **Drawdown Thresholds:** Use limits, e.g. if you are down 10% below the equity high, cut position sizes. No more 10–15% positions. Instead 5–7.5%. Smaller size = fewer errors and far less emotional pressure.
    
6. **Reduce Trading Frequency:** In drawdowns, I trade less. Fewer trades mean fewer mistakes, more clarity, and faster recovery. I only take A-setups and ignore everything that’s average or sloppy.
    
7. **Take a Break:** If you are down 10–15% from the high, take a break for 5–10 days. No trading at all. Distance resets your emotions and gives you the clean perspective you can’t access when you’re in the middle of a losing phase.
    
8. **Have a [[market trend model]]:** I only trade aggressively when my model is green. In yellow I become selective. In red I reduce exposure close to zero. Bad market conditions are the biggest driver of deep drawdowns — a trend model protects you.
    
9. **Capital Preservation Mindset:** My goal is not to make money every day. My goal is to keep my equity curve near the highs. The smaller the drawdown, the fewer gains you need to reach a new high. That’s pure math — and it changes everything.
    
10. **Avoid [[don't trade emotionally]]:** In drawdowns, I never try to “make it back.” No revenge trades. No “one last try.” This is where discipline matters. I rely on my rules, not my emotions. Structure saves me when it gets tough.
