### Key Industry Classifications to Follow

If you are building a watchlist or a screening tool, these are the three lists professional analysts use to find that "granular" data:

#### 1. GICS (Global Industry Classification Standard)

- **Best for:** Stock market analysis and global comparison.
    
- **Structure:** 11 Sectors → 25 Industry Groups → 74 Industries → **163 Sub-Industries**.
    
- **Granular Example:** Instead of "Utilities," GICS gives you **"Renewable Electricity"** vs. **"Multi-Utilities."**
    

#### 2. NAICS (North American Industry Classification System)

- **Best for:** Deep dive into private companies or specific manufacturing processes.
    
- **Structure:** Uses 6-digit codes.
    
- **Granular Example:** * `335911`: Storage Battery Manufacturing.
    
    - `336611`: Ship Building and Repairing.
        

#### 3. ICB (Industry Classification Benchmark)

- **Best for:** European and FTSE-listed stocks (similar to GICS).
    
- **Granular Example:** It separates **"Electronic Equipment: Gauges and Controls"** from **"Electronic Equipment: Control Systems."**
    

### How to use this for Analysis

When you use a terminal (like Bloomberg or FactSet) or a free screener (like Finviz or Yahoo Finance), look for the **"Industry"** filter rather than the **"Sector"** filter.

- **Sector:** Industrials
    
- **Industry:** Marine Shipping
    
- **Granular Sub-Industry:** Tanker Shipping vs. Container Shipping
    

> **Analyst Tip:** For your specific interest in **Batteries**, you often have to look in two places: **Electrical Equipment** (for industrial/utility batteries) and **Auto Parts** (for EV battery manufacturers).
