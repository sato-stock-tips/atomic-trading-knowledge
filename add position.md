### never add to losers

We never average down because we aim to limit our losses. Adding to our losers or averaging down by buying on the way down to “cheapen” the average cost, is antithetical to our method. All good traders average up, not average down.

### adding to winners

adding to winners is like adding a completely new position atomically. it has to satisfy either surf trades, turf trades, or rebirth trades. the prospect of adding to winners make turf trades appealing. when a turf trades breaks out and advances into surf trade mode, it allows for adding to winners. to an extent rebirth trades allow the same. surf trades however, offer limited chances to add to winners

do not layer surf trades onto each other on the same trend advancement. that is adding risk unnecesary

the first purchase of a stock should be the largest and no subsequent purchase after should be larger than the initial purchase. hence why the first purchase should not have [[wide stops]], allowing us to put on the largest size possible

the second purchase (first add) should be no larger than half the first purchase, the third purchase (final add) should be no larger than half the second purchase, i.e. 100 stocks, 50 stocks, 25 stocks in that manner

the reason being that adding half or less of the current [[position size]] ensures that the average buy point does not creep up and erode the unrealized profits

amateurs often add larger and larger size as the position goes their way. this is perilous and can result in a winning position become a break-even or worse still - a losing position
