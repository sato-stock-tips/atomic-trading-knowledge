The hardest skill in trading is not finding winners - it's keeping them. Most traders cut winners too early and let losers run too long. This inverts the [[R-multiples]] equation and destroys equity curves.

## Qullamaggie's Philosophy

"Your edge isn't in being right more often. Your edge is in making more when you're right than you lose when you're wrong. One 50%+ winner pays for five 7% losses. But only if you let it run."

**The Math:**
```
Scenario A: Cut winners at +15%, hold losers to -8%
Win Rate: 60%
6 wins × 15% = +90%
4 losses × -8% = -32%
Net: +58% across 10 trades

Scenario B: Let winners run to +50%, cut losers at -7%
Win Rate: 40%
4 wins × 50% = +200%
6 losses × -7% = -42%
Net: +158% across 10 trades

Lower win rate, MUCH better results.
```

This is the power of [[assymetric bets]].

## How to Let Winners Run

### 1. Trail Stops Using Moving Averages

**Qullamaggie's EMA Ladder:**

**For strong surf trades adhering to EMA 10:**
- Initial stop: below entry or EMA 10
- As stock advances: trail stop to previous day's low OR EMA 10, whichever is higher
- Never tighten stop unless EMA 10 rises
- This allows maximum room for the move

**For established positions:**
- After stock proves itself (>10% profit), move to EMA 20
- Trail stop to EMA 20 as long as trend intact
- This gives even more room for extended moves
- Only exit if closes BELOW EMA 20

**Integration with [[sell into weakness]]:**
- Sell 2/3 if price closes below EMA 10 on volume
- Sell final 1/3 if price closes below EMA 20
- This ensures you capture most of the move while protecting against [[breakdown]]

### 2. Use the 7-11 Take Profit Rule

Per [[69 take profit rule]]:

**Scale out at ATR% multiples from SMA 50:**
- At 7× ATR%: Sell 20% of position
- At 8× ATR%: Sell 20% of position
- At 9× ATR%: Sell 20% of position
- At 10× ATR%: Sell 20% of position
- At 11× ATR%: Sell remaining 20%

**Why this works:**
- You're taking profits into strength (not weakness)
- You stay in the trade for the full move
- You avoid the pain of watching +50% turn into +10%
- You lock in gains systematically, not emotionally

**Example:**
```
Stock with 5% ATR%, SMA 50 at $100
7× ATR% = $135 (35% gain) - Sell 20%
8× ATR% = $140 (40% gain) - Sell 20%
9× ATR% = $145 (45% gain) - Sell 20%
10× ATR% = $150 (50% gain) - Sell 20%
11× ATR% = $155 (55% gain) - Sell 20%

Average exit: ~$145 (+45% gain)
If you sold too early at +20%, you left +25% on table
```

### 3. Recognize the Staircase Pattern

From [[how stocks move like staircase]]:

**Winners don't go straight up:**
- They advance → consolidate → advance → consolidate
- Each consolidation is an opportunity for the weak hands to exit
- Each consolidation resets the [[extended stocks]] meter
- Professional traders hold through these consolidations

**During consolidations:**
- [[volume]] dries up (watch [[relative volume at time]] <0.8)
- Stock builds [[tight base]] near EMAs
- Price may touch EMA 20 or even SMA 50
- This is normal, healthy, and necessary for next leg

**Don't confuse consolidation with [[breakdown]]:**
- Consolidation: low volume, holds above EMAs
- Breakdown: high volume, breaks below EMAs
- Hold through first, exit on second

### 4. The Pocket Pivot Exception

Per [[pocket pivot]] rule:

**When stock has explosive +10%+ day on high volume:**
- This may be a climax move
- Sell 10% of position into the strength
- Trail stop on remaining position
- Protects against immediate reversal
- But still lets you capture more upside if it continues

**Why only 10%?**
- Qullamaggie teaches that many pocket pivots lead to MORE upside
- The +10% day might be the start of parabolic phase, not the end
- Selling 10% satisfies psychological need to "do something"
- Keeping 90% ensures you don't miss the real move

### 5. Position Building on Winners

From [[add position]]:

**When winners prove themselves:**
- Initial position: 100 shares
- First add (after +15-20% profit): 50 shares max
- Second add (after +30-40% profit): 25 shares max

**Why add to winners?**
- Concentrates capital in what's working
- Proves institutional sponsorship through price action
- Leverages [[relative strength]]
- Builds larger total position in leaders

**Critical rules:**
- NEVER add more than initial position size
- NEVER add to losers (averaging down forbidden)
- Each add must qualify as new [[surf trade]], [[turf trade]], or [[rebirth trade]] setup
- Use [[turf trade]] clumped MA reclaims as add points

**Position building example:**
```
Entry: 100 shares at $50 ($5,000)
First add: 50 shares at $60 after turf trade reclaim ($3,000)
Second add: 25 shares at $75 after surf trade pullback ($1,875)

Total: 175 shares, average cost $56.43
If exits at $100:
Profit = 175 × ($100 - $56.43) = $7,625 (77% return on $9,875 invested)

If had never added:
Profit = 100 × ($100 - $50) = $5,000 (100% return on $5,000 invested)

Adding increased absolute dollars while decreasing percentage return
But absolute dollars pay the bills, not percentages
```

### 6. Ignore Short-Term Noise

**Qullamaggie's perspective:**
- Intraday wiggles don't matter
- What matters: daily closes relative to EMAs
- A stock can be down -3% intraday but close +1% - that's bullish
- Don't get shaken out by normal volatility

**Use [[ATR%]] for context:**
- Stock with 6% ATR% can easily swing +/-3% intraday
- This is normal, not a sell signal
- Only daily closes below EMAs matter
- Intraday movement is noise for swing traders

### 7. The Weekly Time Frame

**Check weekly charts:**
- Weekly uptrends last much longer than daily
- Stock can have 2-3 weak daily sessions but weekly trend intact
- Weekly EMA 10 and 20 provide major support
- Think in weeks and months, not days

**Weekly perspective prevents premature exits:**
- Daily chart: "Oh no, it's pulling back!"
- Weekly chart: "Normal consolidation in strong uptrend"
- This context helps you hold

## Common Mistakes That Cut Winners Short

### Mistake 1: Taking Profits Too Early

**The Trap:**
- Stock up +15%, you sell "to lock in gains"
- Stock continues to +50%
- You made +15%, left +35% on table
- Do this 10 times, you've destroyed your [[equity curve]]

**The Fix:**
- Use [[69 take profit rule]] systematically
- Don't exit based on feelings
- Let the stock tell you when to exit via EMA breaks

### Mistake 2: Round-Tripping Winners

From [[sell into strength]]:

**The Trap:**
- Stock goes from +30% to +50%
- You don't sell any
- Stock reverses back to +10%
- You "didn't make anything" emotionally

**The Fix:**
- Scale out at ATR% multiples
- Sell small amounts into vertical moves
- Lock in SOME gains while staying in trend
- Avoid complete round-trips

### Mistake 3: Selling on Minor Pullbacks

**The Trap:**
- Stock pulls back -5% from highs
- You panic sell "to protect gains"
- Stock resumes uptrend next day
- You sold the exact bottom of healthy pullback

**The Fix:**
- Only sell on EMA violations, not price pullbacks
- Price can pull back 10-15% and still hold EMA 20
- That's a healthy [[pullback]], not a [[breakdown]]
- Use moving averages, not price, as exit criteria

### Mistake 4: Exiting on Profit Target

**The Trap:**
- "I'll sell at +20% profit"
- Stock hits +20%, you sell
- Stock continues to +60%
- Arbitrary targets cap your upside

**The Fix:**
- No profit targets
- Exit based on price action (EMA breaks) or extension ([[69 take profit rule]])
- Let the market decide when trend is over
- Your job is to follow, not predict

## Position Management Summary

**Initial entry (100% position):**
- Tight stop 0.5-1.0× ATR% below entry
- Full size since stop is tight

**After +10% profit:**
- Move stop to break-even or EMA 10
- Consider first add if proper setup appears

**After +20% profit:**
- Trail stop to EMA 20
- House money, let it run
- Consider second add if proper setup appears

**After 7× ATR% from SMA 50:**
- Begin scaling out 20% at each ATR% increment
- Trail stop on remaining position
- Lock in profits systematically

**On EMA 10 break (with volume):**
- Sell 2/3 per [[sell into weakness]]
- Trail stop on final 1/3 at EMA 20

**On EMA 20 break:**
- Sell remaining 1/3
- Exit completely
- Wait for new setup
