# Markup

**Markup** is the stage where price advances in a sustained uptrend after completing [[institutional accumulation]]. This is Stage 2 in the market cycle — the phase where the biggest gains are made.

## The Uptrend Phase

From [[follow through day]]:
> "It signals the end of the [[shakeout]] phase and the start of a potential markup phase in the broader market cycle."

Markup begins when institutions have finished accumulating and actively drive price higher. This is when [[trending market]] conditions favor aggressive long positioning.

## Stage 2 Characteristics

From [[rebirth trade]]:
> "This is when the stock is undergoing a Stan Weinstein Stage Analysis, stage two - price is advancing upwards."

**Stan Weinstein's Stage 2 Framework:**
- Stage 2A: Bullish Trend established
- Stage 2B: [[breakout]] confirmed
- Stage 2C: [[extended stocks]] (>7x [[ATR%]] from 50 SMA)
- Stage 2D: Exhausted (>11x ATR% from 50 SMA)

## The Engine of Big Moves

From [[five star setups]]:
> "Prior Power Move: The stock must be up 70% or more in the last advance. Big breakouts only work if institutions already proved their interest with real price movement."

Markup creates the prior power move that validates [[relative strength]]. Without a strong markup phase, there's no proven institutional demand.

## Qullamaggie's Perspective

From [[pullback]]:
> "Surf trade is when a stock with tremendous [[relative strength]] takes a breather and comes back to the [[key moving averages]] that's guiding them."

During markup, the best entries come on [[pullback]]s to key moving averages — buying consolidations within the uptrend rather than chasing [[extended stocks]].

## Mark Minervini's Trend Template

Markup phase requires:
- Price above rising EMA 10, EMA 20, 50 SMA, 150 SMA, 200 SMA
- Moving averages aligned (shorter above longer)
- Strong [[relative strength]] vs market
- [[volume]] expansion on up days

## Trading During Markup

This is the phase to be most aggressive:
- Add to winning positions using [[add position]] strategy
- Allow wider [[stop loss]] levels to survive normal volatility
- Use [[pullback]] entries like [[surf trade]] to enter existing leaders
- Focus on [[industry (previously known as sectors)]] leaders with the strongest [[relative strength]]

Markup is where fortunes are made. The key is identifying it early through [[episodic pivots]] and riding it until signs of [[distribution]] appear.
