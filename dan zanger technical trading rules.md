1. Trade chart patterns, not opinions.

Bull flags, cup-with-handles, flat bases, and ascending triangles are your entire playbook. If the stock isn't forming a recognizable pattern with clear entry and stop levels, move on. No pattern, no trade.

2. Volume is the lie detector.

A breakout without volume is a fake-out. Demand at least 150-200% of average daily volume on the breakout day. Stocks that break out on light volume routinely fail and trap buyers.

3. Buy the breakout on the first day - not the second or third.

The best entries happen the moment the stock clears the pattern's resistance on heavy volume. Chasing a stock that's already extended 5-10% beyond the breakout destroys your risk/reward and puts your stop too far away.

4. Cut losses fast and without negotiation.

If a breakout fails and the stock drops back into the base, you're wrong. Accept it. Losses of 5-8% are the cost of doing business. Turning small losses into big ones is the only unrecoverable mistake.

5. Trade in the direction of the market.

Even the best chart patterns fail in a downtrending market. When the Nasdaq or S&P is breaking down, reduce position size dramatically or go to cash entirely. Bull flags don't work in bear markets.

6. Focus on stocks in leading sectors.

Sector rotation drives the market. The stocks that make the biggest moves are almost always in the hottest industry groups of that cycle - tech, biotech, energy, whatever the institutions are piling into. Trade where the money is flowing.

7. Let winners run using trailing stops.

Once a stock breaks out and moves in your favor, trail your stop using the 10-day or 20-day moving average. Don't sell a winning stock just because you're up 15%. The goal is to catch moves of 50-100%+ by staying in the trend.

8. Tighten up in choppy, range-bound markets.

When the market isn't trending cleanly, breakouts have a much higher failure rate. Trade smaller size, take quicker profits, and be more selective. Choppy markets eat traders who trade full size.

9. Study charts obsessively - hundreds per day.

Pattern recognition is a skill that only develops through repetition. The more charts you study, the faster you spot setups and the better your instinct becomes for separating clean patterns from sloppy ones.

10. Never average down on a losing trade.

Adding to a loser is hoping, not trading. If your thesis was right, the stock would be going up. Averaging down increases your exposure to a stock that's already proving you wrong.
