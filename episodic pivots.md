## The Purpose: Capturing the "Shift in Narrative"

The primary purpose of an Episodic Pivot is to identify the **start of a new, sustained [[trending market]]. Most technical patterns focus on price action alone, but the EP seeks to capture the moment when a significant piece of news—a catalyst—changes the market's perception of a company's future earnings power.

By entering at the point of an EP, a trader is looking to capture **Alpha** by riding the wave of institutional "big money" that takes days, weeks, or months to build a full position. The goal is to find stocks that have the potential to move 50%, 100%, or more in a relatively short period, ie to form a [[high tight flag]]

---

## The Qualifying Conditions of an Episodic Pivot

For a price move to qualify as a true Episodic Pivot under Bonde’s methodology, it must satisfy four distinct "pillars." If one is missing, it is likely just a common breakout rather than a true structural pivot.

### 1. The Catalyst (The "Episodic" Element)

An EP must be triggered by a discrete event. This is the "why" behind the move. Common catalysts include:

- **Earnings Surprises:** Beating estimates significantly and raising future guidance. [[eps & sales]] focus.
    
- **New Products/Contracts:** A breakthrough technology or a massive partnership.
    
- **Regulatory Approval:** Such as an FDA approval for a biotech firm.
    
- **Industry Shifts:** A macro change that suddenly makes a niche [[industry (previously known as sectors)]] highly profitable.
    

### 2. The Price Gap

The stock must **[[gaps]] up** significantly. This gap represents a "shock" to the system where demand completely overwhelms supply. A gap shows that market participants are so eager to get in that they are willing to pay a price much higher than the previous day's close. Usually, a gap of 5% or more is a baseline, though the most powerful EPs often gap 10-20%+.

### 3. Explosive Volume (The Institutional Footprint)

[[volume]] is the most critical confirmation of an EP. It validates that the move is being driven by institutions (pension funds, hedge funds) rather than retail traders. relevant: [[simple volume]]

- **The Requirement:** The volume on the day of the pivot should be at least **3x to 10x the Average Daily Volume (ADV)**.
    
- This "Max Relative Volume" indicates that a massive exchange of hands is occurring, creating a new "floor" for the stock.
  
- Should hit [[pocket pivot]]
    

### 4. Range and Close

The price action on the day of the pivot must remain strong. An EP fails if the stock "fades" (opens high and closes near the bottom of the day's range). Ideally, the stock should close in the **upper 25% of its daily range**, forming a large, green "Marubozu" or wide-range candle. This demonstrates that the buying pressure remained relentless throughout the entire trading session.

## The Mechanics: Entry and Risk Management

Trading an Episodic Pivot requires a shift in mindset; while most traders are afraid to "buy high," the EP trader understands they are buying the **strength** that leads to even higher prices.

- **The Entry:** Traders typically enter near the end of the day if the conditions are met, or on a 1-minute or 5-minute **[[opening range breakout]]* once the initial volatility settles.
    
- **The [[stop loss]]:** Risk is strictly managed. The most common stop-loss levels are the **Low of the Day (LOD)** or the bottom of the opening gap. If the stock falls back into the gap, the "structural shift" has failed, and the trader exits immediately.
    
- **The Hold:** Because the EP marks the start of a trend, these positions are often held as long as the stock remains above [[key moving averages]].

### Expanded EP Family (Variations He Trades)
Over time he expanded beyond the “classical” earnings/sales EP:
- **Turnaround EP**: Beaten-down stock with credible recovery in earnings/profitability; often longer-duration trends.
- **Story/Thematic EP**: Narrative or sector-theme driven (can produce some of the largest % moves but more speculative).
- **EP 9 Million (Volume EP)**: Objective volume filter—any stock trading 9 million+ shares in a day (far above its norm). Volume itself is the signal that something significant is happening; useful even if the full story isn’t immediately clear. He trades these on both long and short sides.
- **Delayed Reaction EP (Long or Short)**: When the catalyst day is messy (big gap + fade or volatility), log it and wait days to weeks for secondary confirmation (e.g., red-to-green reversal, breakout from tight consolidation/range, reclaim of key level on volume). These often allow larger position sizes and better-defined risk because the initial chaos has settled. Same logic applies to negative catalysts for shorts (wait for failed bounce).
- **Sugar Babies**: A curated basket of names that repeatedly show short, explosive EP-style bursts (e.g., 40–50% in 5–6 days). Treat their volume breakouts or pullbacks into support as recurring EP opportunities.
