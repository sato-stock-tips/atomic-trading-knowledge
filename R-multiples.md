## **The Compounding Edge: Thinking in R-Multiples**

_Most traders focus on win rate. I think in R-Multiples._ _R is your unit of risk (e.g. 1% from your trading capital). If you risk 1R on a trade and gain 3R, that's a 3R winner. Once you start tracking trades in R, you'll see the power of compounding and why big winners matter more than hit rate._

1. **Define R Clearly:** R = the fixed amount you're willing to lose on a trade. My R is 0.15 to 0.3. There has been research that shown that 0.5 is way too high because with tight stops, 1 or 2 position takes up almost all the equity in the portfolio
    
2. **Normalize Results:** A $500 loss and a $2,000 win look different, but in R both might be -1R and +4R. This makes your trading results comparable across all setups.
    
3. **Shift from Win Rate to Payoff Ratio:** A 40% win rate with an average +3R payoff is better than a 70% win rate with +0.5R payoff. The math always wins.
    
4. **Track Every Trade in R:** Don't just log dollar PnL. Record entry, stop, exit, and the R-multiple result. Patterns will emerge.
    
5. **Study Your Distribution:** Most traders lose because they cut winners. Your curve depends on a few +8R or +10R trades. Identify them. Protect them.
    
6. **Guard Against Roundtrips:** Never let a +3R trade turn into breakeven. Move stops, protect cushions, and make sure you bank R when it's on the table.
    
7. **Build Conviction With Data:** After 50-100 trades logged in R, you'll know your true edge. It's no longer hope — it's math.
    
8. **Think Portfolio-Level:** R-multiples let you evaluate strategy performance across different market conditions, not just single trades.
    
9. **Scale Winners, Not Losers:** Add size only when you've got cushion (already in +R territory). Never throw more risk into a -1R loser.
    
10. **Focus on Compounding, Not Action:** One or two big winners per quarter can make your year. Your job is to stay in the game long enough to catch them.
