Definition of technical analysis as the study of market action through charts to forecast future price trends, emphasizing price, volume, and time over fundamental factors (Schabacker)

- Technical approach prioritizes the market's own behavior as the most important factor, distinguishing it from fundamental analysis which focuses on intrinsic value (Schabacker)
- 
- Market price reflects all known information, including hopes, fears, and insider knowledge, making it superior for practical trading decisions (Edwards & Magee)
- 
- Stock prices tend to move in trends, with volume confirming the trend, and established trends likely to continue (Edwards & Magee)
- 
- Technical analysis records trading history graphically to deduce probable future trends, acknowledging that past patterns can indicate future behavior (Edwards & Magee)
- 
- Distinction between major, intermediate, and minor movements in market trends, with major trends being the most significant for long-term forecasting (Schabacker)
- 
- Advantages of self-maintained charts for closer market observation and timely shifts in focus among stocks (Schabacker)
- 
- Technical factors outweigh fundamentals in short- to intermediate-term trading due to market psychology (Schabacker)
