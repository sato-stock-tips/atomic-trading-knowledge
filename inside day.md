An inside day is a price action pattern in trading that occurs when the entire trading range of the current day (its high and low) is completely contained within the trading range of the previous day.

In other words both of these must be true at the same time: Today's high is less than or equal to yesterday's high Today's low is greater than or equal to yesterday's low This means today's candle including its wicks fits entirely inside yesterday's candle range on a daily chart. The pattern shows a clear drop in volatility and market indecision. After a directional move the market pauses buyers and sellers reach temporary balance and neither side can push price outside the previous day's extremes. It often acts as consolidation or a coil before a larger breakout move either continuing the prior trend or occasionally reversing it.

Traders usually trade inside days with a breakout strategy: Wait for the inside day to complete Place a buy stop order just above the previous day's high to catch an upside breakout
Place a sell stop order just below the previous day's low to catch a downside breakout

Many traders only take the trade in the direction of the bigger trend for higher probability

[[stop loss]] is typically placed on the opposite side of the previous day's range or slightly beyond

Profit targets can be previous swing points a measured move or a fixed reward to risk ratio such as 2:1 or 3:1

False breakouts happen so context matters a lot trend direction nearby support and resistance levels volume and overall market structure all improve the odds.

Inside days appear often especially after big trend days before major news events weekends or holidays when volume is lower and ranges tighten.

In short an inside day is a quiet narrow range day completely inside the previous day's range it signals the calm before a potential breakout move and is one of the simplest setups price action traders watch for.
