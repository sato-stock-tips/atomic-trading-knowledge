

The Ants indicator is based on the research of David Ryan, three-time winner of the U.S. Investing Championship1 David came up with the idea for the indicator while managing the New USA Growth Fund at William O'Neil + Company. David was interested to understand what drove some stocks higher once they were extended from their most recent base, while others had only moderate moves up.

What David found during his research was that stocks making the biggest moves often had consistent buying on [[volume]] over a period of 12 to 15 trading days. Stocks with these characteristics may be under institutional accumulation, where it may take days to weeks to fill a position5.

**— Momentum, Volume And Price (MVP) —**

The Ants indicator looks for the following:

- **Momentum** - The stock is up at least 12 of the past 15 days.
    
- **Volume** - The volume has increased over the past 15 days by 20% to 25%.
    
- **Price** - The price is up at least 20% over the past 15 days.
    

**— Why Is The Indicator Called Ants? —**

The "Ants" reference comes from the original implementation at William O'Neil where small black marks were placed above price bars when the MVP criteria were met.

**— Ant Colors —**

When the indicator was created at William O'Neil, Ants were shown only when all three MVP requirements had been met10. You may find it helpful to see Ants when momentum and volume or momentum and price are detected11. With that in mind, the Ants indicator supports the following:

- **Gray Ants** - Momentum requirement met.
    
- **Blue Ants** - Momentum and price requirements met.
    
- **Yellow Ants** - Momentum and volume requirements met.
    
- **Green Ants** - Momentum, volume and price requirements met.
    

Important: the Ants indicator is showed above each trading bar

**— What Does It Mean If More Than 15 Ants Are Shown? —**

It is possible that two or more consecutive series of Ants overlap. For example, you may have one series of 15 bars that meet all the MVP requirements. Somewhere within that series, another run of 15 bars may begin that also meets the MVP requirements. In situations such as this, once each run of 15 Ants is displayed, the total number of consecutive Ants shown will be greater than 15.

David Ryan’s ANTS (or “Ants”) Indicator is a technical signal he developed to identify strong institutional accumulation in stocks. It flags periods where big money (mutual funds, hedge funds, etc.) is steadily buying over multiple days or weeks rather than in a single spike.

Why “Ants”?

While managing money at William O’Neil & Co., Ryan marked qualifying price bars with small dots above them. These dots stacked up and looked like a trail of ants marching along the chart — hence the name.0

Exact Criteria (Daily Chart Basis)

The indicator triggers when all three conditions are met within a rolling 15-trading-day window (roughly 3 calendar weeks):

- Price: +20–25% gain from the start of the period
- Volume: +20–25% increase in volume over the same period
- Momentum: At least 12 out of the 15 days closed higher (some implementations use slight variations like 11+ up days in a 13-day window)

This combination shows persistent, high-quality demand — exactly what institutions need when building large positions without spiking the price too fast against themselves.30

How It Works on the Weekly Timeframe (Your Specific Question)

The classic ANTS calculation is daily (15 trading days). David Ryan himself is a strong advocate of weekly charts for the big picture because they cut through daily noise and better reveal institutional behavior, major bases, and the true trend.

Here’s how ANTS integrates with weekly timeframe analysis:

1. ANTS as an Early Accumulation Signal (Daily → Weekly Context)

- ANTS often appears on the daily chart during or right after a strong advance.
- Switch to the weekly chart to see the larger structure. The 3-week ANTS surge usually shows up as 2–4 strong weekly bars with good closes and supporting volume.
- This tells you institutions are “in” and the stock has fuel for further upside — often forming multiple bases (“stairs”) as it climbs.

3. Weekly Chart Role After ANTS Appears

- Base Formation / Tightening: After the initial ANTS surge, many stocks consolidate or “tighten” into a new base or handle. The weekly chart makes these larger consolidations obvious (e.g., a cup-with-handle, flat base, or high-tight flag forming over several weeks).
- Entry Timing: Wait for a proper pivot breakout on the weekly chart (new high on increased weekly volume) after the ANTS signal. This is lower-risk than jumping in the middle of the ANTS surge itself.
- Trend Confirmation: Use weekly moving averages (commonly 10-week or 30-week) and overall stage analysis. ANTS stocks that stay above rising weekly MAs have higher odds of continuing higher.

5. Multi-Leg Advances

- Stocks showing ANTS frequently make big moves in stages. The weekly chart helps you see each new “leg up” and the bases in between. Ryan has noted that once institutions are accumulating steadily, the stock can keep working higher even when it looks extended on the daily chart.

7. Adapted ANTS on Weekly Charts (Advanced)

- Some TradingView/community scripts (developed with Ryan’s input or faithful recreations) allow the indicator on all timeframes, including weekly. On weekly bars it looks for analogous strength over ~3 weekly periods (majority up weeks + price/volume surge). These are useful but secondary to the original daily logic.

Best Entry Technique for Riding the Weekly Upward Wave as a Momentum Trader

The highest-edge approach combines daily Ants as the institutional fuel detector with weekly chart structure as the map and pivot breakout (or high-probability pullback entry) as the tactical trigger. This is not “buy every green ant and hold weekly.” It is: identify accumulation, wait for the market to confirm the next leg via structure and renewed demand, then participate with defined risk while letting the weekly trend do the heavy lifting for continuation.

Step 1: Screening and Candidate Selection (Daily Ants + Filters)  
Run a daily scan for full green Ants (or building partial signals as early alerts). Prioritize names already in or emerging from a base rather than deep vertical extensions. Layer your existing tools: relative strength (stock vs. SPX or sector over multiple months), price above rising weekly moving averages (10-week primary; 30-week for longer context), and any fundamental quick-check (earnings/sales acceleration preferred; at minimum no major red flags). For your 15% sleeve, focus on liquid names where volume data is reliable—strongest validation exists in US growth/momentum names; Korean large-caps can be adapted if volume is clean, but institutional dynamics differ and the signal’s edge may be lower—test explicitly. Limit to 4–6 positions or size so individual risk remains small.

Step 2: Weekly Chart Context (The Wave Map)  
Switch to weekly. Confirm the primary uptrend (higher highs/lows series, price holding above rising 10-week MA). Locate the Ants cluster: the ideal setup is Ants appearing during or shortly after a base breakout or early in a leg, followed by consolidation—tightening ranges, shallow pullbacks to the 10-week MA or prior pivot on declining relative volume, or classic formations (flat base, high-tight flag, or handle). This consolidation phase is where weak hands exit and supply tightens, setting up the next impulse.

Avoid or deprioritize if weekly shows distribution (high-volume down weeks, failed breakouts that reverse, or price breaking key MAs). Ants in a vertical parabolic move without consolidation is lower quality for continuation—it often marks the end of that leg rather than the start of a new one. The weekly view reveals whether the Ants fuel is part of a larger multi-month wave or an isolated surge.

Step 3: Entry Triggers (Ranked by Edge for Momentum Continuation)

- Highest probability / best risk-reward core entry: After Ants-driven advance and subsequent weekly consolidation, buy the pivot breakout—new weekly high on expanding volume (higher than recent weeks). Stop below the consolidation low or under the 10-week MA. This is the classic O’Neil/Ryan-style timing: Ants confirms institutional sponsorship and fuel; the breakout confirms demand has resumed after digestion. It captures the next leg of the weekly wave with a relatively tight, defined risk point. Examples like the HOOD sequence (Ants → tight base → breakout to new highs) fit this template precisely.
- Aggressive momentum participation (ride the current wave strength): On the daily chart, enter or scale in when Ants (full or building) coincides with a pullback to support (20/50-day MA, prior Ants cluster zone, or rising trendline) followed by price strength—close above a recent daily high or with volume confirmation. Initial size smaller (e.g., half position); add on confirmation or successful retest. Use this when the weekly trend is clearly intact and you want exposure during or right after the accumulation signature itself. Higher short-term volatility but captures more of an ongoing leg.
- Hybrid scale-in for the 15% sleeve: Deploy initial capital on daily Ants + relative strength confirmation during a support test. Add to full size only on the weekly pivot breakout or after the position holds the 10-week MA for 1–2 weeks with no breakdown. This balances early entry with structural confirmation and fits a dedicated continuation book.

Do not treat Ants itself as the immediate buy trigger in most cases—especially if the stock is already extended from its most recent base. The signal often occurs mid-to-late in a leg; chasing it directly increases the chance of buying the top of that specific move.

Step 4: Position Management and Exits to Actually Ride the Wave  
Initial risk: Size so a stop below the weekly consolidation low or 10-week MA risks a small portfolio percentage (align with your 0.2% 1R discipline or scale appropriately for the sleeve). Trail stops upward: move to breakeven after a solid gain (10-15%) or 2–3 weeks of progress. Then trail below rising weekly swing lows or the 10-week MA. For strong trends, you can be looser than pure daily momentum trading—this is where the weekly timeframe helps. Add to winners on dips to the 10-week MA only if relative strength holds and Ants or accumulation signs reappear.

Exit rules: Weekly close below the 10-week MA on volume; clear distribution (heavy down weeks); failed pivot that reverses; or Ants cluster dissipates while price action deteriorates. Do not marry the position—momentum continuation requires the structure and fuel to remain intact. Harvest or reduce on strength if the broader market regime shifts (e.g., SPX breaking its own 10/30-week supports).
