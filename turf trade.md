Turf trades is a setup where price is hovering around clumped-up [[mark minervini (key moving averages)]]. The key moving averages are the  EMA 10, EMA 20, and SMA 50. When they are all very close to each other (clumped up), and the price has reclaimed these three key moving averages in the clumped-up MA setup.

it takes time for the key moving averages (the EMA 10, EMA 20, and the SMA 50) to all clump up together or get close to each other. What I have noticed is that price when it's flagging in the consolidation zone of the high tight flag, it would test the EMA 20 once, and then test the SMA 50 once, before it starts to take off again on another leg up. This is very similar to [[how stocks move like staircase]]

Conditions:
This has to come after a [[high tight flag]], which is a huge price move up, a huge run up, and then perhaps a drop into a base where price is consolidating within a base of preferably three months long. 

When the price reclaims all three key moving averages in one session or two sessions, then the price tends to shoot up to the top of the range within only a few trading sessions. So even if the breakout from the flag consolidation area was a failure, the price action would still be positive enough, such that a trader could walk away with high returns of [[R-multiples]], having bought at a clumped-up base at the base where key moving averages are clumped up and [[sell into strength]] or into a breakout failure.

So the ideal entry is when the price reclaims all three key moving averages, the EMA 10, EMA 20, and the SMA 50. This usually comes with an ema crossover as well
