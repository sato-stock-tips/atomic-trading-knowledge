# Qullamaggie's Definition and Conditions of a High Tight Flag

Kristjan Kullamägi (Qullamaggie) is a prominent swing trader known for his momentum-based strategies, often shared through his Twitch streams, website, and X (formerly Twitter) posts. While he draws inspiration from classic patterns like those described by William O'Neil (e.g., in *How to Make Money in Stocks*), Qullamaggie adapts and emphasizes variations that align with his high-probability, risk-reward focused approach. The "high tight flag" (HTF) is not one of his three core "timeless [[setups]]" (which include [[episodic pivots]], [[breakout]], and [[surf trade]] aka pullbacks to moving averages), but he frequently references it as a powerful continuation pattern, especially in the context of breakouts. He describes it as a rare, bullish setup that signals potential for explosive upside, often occurring after a sharp advance.

Below is an exhaustive breakdown based on Qullamaggie's own explanations from his streams, website, X posts, and related discussions (e.g., on Reddit's r/qullamaggie). Note that Qullamaggie often uses the term interchangeably with similar patterns like "tight flags," "power plays," or intraday "Holy Grail" setups, focusing on practical application over rigid definitions.

## Definition
A high tight flag is a bullish [[continuation]] that forms after a significant upward price surge (the "flagpole"), followed by a brief, orderly consolidation (the "flag") with low volatility and tightening price action. Qullamaggie views it as a variation of his [[breakout]] setup, where the stock "rests" or "surfs" along rising moving averages before resuming its uptrend with a range expansion (breakout). 

- **Key Characteristics**:
  - **High**: Refers to the steep, vertical flagpole formed by a rapid price increase (80% or more in a short period).
  - **Tight**: The flag is narrow, with a contracting range, [[higher lows]], and minimal volatility, making it "tight" rather than "loose" (wide-ranging).
  - **Flag**: A consolidation phase that looks like a small rectangle, channel, symmetrical triangle, or descending triangle on the chart.
  
Qullamaggie notes that on intraday timeframes (e.g., 1-minute or 5-minute charts), this is akin to the "Holy Grail" setup, while on daily charts, it's explicitly called a high tight flag. He emphasizes that it's about finding "tight, high-probability areas to enter" for 5-20x risk-reward trades.

- **Tight vs. Loose Flags**: A tight flag narrows progressively toward the tip/breakout point, indicating building pressure for an upside move. A loose flag has a wider range and is earlier in formation (e.g., middle of the consolidation). 

## Conditions
Qullamaggie is exhaustive in requiring multiple layers of confirmation for a valid high tight flag, blending technical, fundamental, and market context elements. He doesn't adhere strictly to O'Neil's classic rules (e.g., exactly 100% flagpole in 8 weeks), but allows variations while prioritizing [[five star setups]] with high conviction. Below are the core conditions, drawn from his streams, posts, and site:

### 1. **Prior Uptrend (Flagpole)**

   - A strong, explosive upward move: Typically 30-100%+ gain in 1-3 months (or shorter on intraday).
   - Often triggered by an [[episodic pivots]], such as earnings surprises, news, or [[industry (previously known as sectors)]] momentum.
   - High volume during the surge, indicating [[institutional accumulation]].
   - The stock should be a market leader (with strong [[relative strength]], ie.[[RS rating]]): Among the top 1-2% performers over 1-month, 3-month, and 6-month periods.
   - Example: A 500% move in weeks, as in $NKLA.

### 2. **Consolidation Phase (Flag)**

   - **Duration**: 2 weeks to 2 months on daily charts; shorter (days to weeks) on intraday. Qullamaggie prefers longer consolidations for higher-quality setups, where slower moving averages (e.g., 50 SMA) become relevant. See [[big base breakout]]
   - **Price Action**: Orderly pullback with higher lows and a tightening range (narrowing highs and lows), possibly presence of [[inside day]]
   - **Volatility**: Low; correction typically <25% from the peak, with minimal drawdown (e.g., 5-10%).
   - **Support Levels**: Price "surfs" or rests on rising 10-day EMA and 20-day EMA moving averages (MAs) (see [[key moving averages]]. The moving averages should "catch up" to the price without the price dropping far below it.
   - **Shakeouts Allowed**: Minor undercuts of the MA (e.g., 1-day dips) are fine if bought up quickly, as they flush out weak hands and attract shorts to cover.
   - **[[volume]]**: Declining during consolidation, building to a breakout.
   - **Form**: Flat channels, symmetrical triangles, or descending triangles. Must be "clean" – no messy wiggles.
   - **Stock Character**: The stock should have a history of strong moves; pay attention to its "personality" (e.g., volatile biotechs vs. steady leaders).

### 3. [[breakout]] (Range Expansion)**
   - Clean break above the flag's upper boundary on increasing volume.
   - Often coincides with a narrative shift (e.g., earnings, sector news).
   - Price above key MAs: 10 > 20 > 50 SMA for alignment.
   - Ideal in strong sectors or after earnings momentum.

### 4. **Additional Contextual Conditions**
   - **Market Environment**: Best in bullish markets; avoid in choppy or bearish conditions.
   - **Star Rating**: Qullamaggie rates setups 1-5 stars; a 5-star HTF has perfect alignment (e.g., tight range, MA support, volume). Even 7-star exceptions exist for exceptional cases.
   - **Sector Strength**: Stock in a leading sector (e.g., biotechs waiting for data).
   - **Risk Management**: [[position size]] to risk 0.15-0.3% of account; tight [[stop loss]] (e.g., below 50 MA at ~5% risk or based on a division of [[ATR%]]).
   - **Variations**: Includes "high tight flag-like" patterns, such as bull flags, pennants, or power plays. Not always a perfect match to O'Neil's (e.g., flagpole <100%).

## Trading the Pattern (Qullamaggie's Rules)
- **Entry**: At breakout, often on opening range highs (first 1-min, 5-min, or 60-min candle highs). For swings, use daily chart. ie. [[opening range breakout]]
- **Stop-Loss**: Below day's low, flag low, or MA; within Average True Range (ATR).
- **[[sell into strength]]**: Scale out 1/3-1/2 after 3-5 days; move stop to breakeven; trail remainder with 10- or 20-day MA (start with 10-day for beginners).]]
- **Avoidances**: Don't [[add position]] ; treat re-flags as new trades. Skip if [[extended stocks]] or narrative unchanged.
