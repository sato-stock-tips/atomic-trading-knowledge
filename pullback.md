# Pullback Trading

A pullback is a temporary price retracement within an established uptrend that provides a lower-risk entry opportunity into a strong stock. Rather than chasing extended stocks, pullback trading allows you to enter proven leaders when they "take a breather" and reset their risk-reward profile.

---

## Qullamaggie's Pullback Concept

**Qullamaggie** refers to pullback trading as one of his three core timeless [[setups]]: the **[[surf trade]]** (pullback to moving averages) and [[rebirth trade]]

### Core Definition

> "Surf trade is when a stock with tremendous [[relative strength]] takes a breather and comes back to the [[key moving averages]] that's guiding them."

The concept centers on stocks having a big run-up with good adherence to a key moving average (EMA 10 or EMA 20). When the stock takes one or two sessions drifting back towards the supporting EMA—ideally within one ATR multiple from the moving average—it provides a high-probability buying opportunity.

### Qullamaggie's Key Conditions

**Condition 1: Volume Dry-Up**
- The price drift back to the supporting EMA must show dry-up in [[volume]]
- Measured by a drop in [[relative volume at time]]
- Low volume on pullbacks = no aggressive selling = healthy retracement

**Condition 2: Not Extended**
- Only enter if the stock is **less than 4x [[ATR%]] from the 50 SMA**
- If [[eps & sales]] fundamentals are exceptional, 5x ATR% from 50 SMA may be tolerated
- This prevents opening [[extended stocks]] and avoids [[entering stocks late]]
- Critical for avoiding #FOMO

**Condition 3: Higher Lows Pattern**
- [[higher lows]] are an integral part of Qullamaggie's approach
- Each pullback should create a higher low than the previous
- Shows accumulation pressure and institutional support
- Best visualized in [[high tight flag]] formations

### Entry Techniques

**If the stock is in a legitimate pullback:**
- IBKR midprice order type preferred
- Market order acceptable for strong setups

**If the stock is breaking out from the pullback:**
- [[opening range breakout]] order type preferred
- Enter on opening range highs (first 1-min, 5-min, or 60-min candle highs)

## Mark Minervini's Moving Average Pullback

**Mark Minervini** emphasizes pullbacks to key moving averages as part of his trend template approach:

### Key Moving Averages for Pullbacks

- **EMA 10** (orange): For very strong stocks showing extreme strength
- **EMA 20** (blue light): For strong stocks in confirmed uptrends
- **50 SMA** (blue): For bigger base formations and longer consolidations

### Minervini's Trend Template Requirements

Before considering a pullback entry, the stock must meet these criteria:

1. Current price above both 150 SMA and 200 SMA
2. 150 SMA above 200 SMA
3. 200 SMA trending up for at least 1 month (preferably 4-5 months)
4. 50 SMA above both 150 SMA and 200 SMA
5. Current price trading above 50 SMA
6. At least 30% above 52-week low (best selections: 100%+ to 300%+)
7. Within 25% of 52-week high (closer to new highs is better)
8. [[RS rating]] no less than 90

> "Strong stocks trade above rising moving averages. If a base forms below those levels, the momentum just isn't there yet." - From [[buying clues]]

## David Ryan's Pullback Principles

**David Ryan**, three-time U.S. Investing Champion, emphasizes discipline in pullback entries:

### Extension Metrics

> "Never buy extended stocks. An extended stock is when the price is more than 4x ATR% from the 50 day simple moving average (SMA)." - From [[extended stocks]]

### Pullback Characteristics

- Wait for [[tight base]] formations after big runs
- Focus on stocks showing **[[higher lows]]** during consolidation
- Light pullbacks on low [[volume]] indicate no aggressive selling
- "Price tends to pop upwards after days of super low volume" - From [[buying clues]]

## Nicolas Darvas' Box Theory Pullbacks

**Nicolas Darvas**, author of "How I Made $2 Million in the Stock Market," contributed a unique perspective on pullbacks within trending structures:

### Darvas Box Pullback Concept

> "Within the range of a box, Darvas considered movement to the bottom of the range a healthy sign. These moves down shakeout weak holders who sell thinking the downward trend may continue." - From [[darvas box]]

- Boxes stack one after another in strong uptrends
- Pullbacks to the bottom of the box are opportunities, not warnings
- Movement is "lively" within boxes—volatility is healthy
- Box breakouts with volume confirm the next leg up

## William O'Neil's Pullback Wisdom

**William O'Neil**, founder of Investor's Business Daily, emphasizes:

### Sector Momentum in Pullbacks

> "Sector momentum is 30% of the move." - Referenced in [[buying clues]]

- Even perfect pullback setups struggle in weak sectors
- Look for **cluster of breakouts** in the same [[industry (previously known as sectors)]]—shows institutional money flow
- "If the entire theme is under pressure, I avoid trading it"

### Volume as Truth Serum

From [[volume]] principles:
- **Low volume + price down** = no real selling ( hold/buy)
- **High volume + price down** = distribution (exit)
- Volume dry-up at lows signals sellers are done—setting up next accumulation phase

## Universal Pullback Principles

### The 12 Signs Framework

From [[buying clues]], a trade-ready pullback requires:

1. **Clear Uptrend:** Price above EMA 20 and EMA 50, both sloping upward
2. **Strong [[relative strength]]:** RS rating >90 or clearly outperforming index
3. **Tight Price Action:** Reduced volatility after strong run
4. **Defined Pattern:** Flat base, flag, or VCP—no messy ranges
5. **[[volume]] Clues:** 1-2 up-days with 1.5-2x average volume in recent weeks
6. **Light Pullbacks:** Down days on low volume—no aggressive selling
7. **Liquidity:** $100M+ average daily turnover
8. **Catalyst Potential:** Earnings, news, or [[industry (previously known as sectors)]] momentum
9. **[[ATR%]] in Range:** 5%+ ATR for movement without excessive risk
10. **Sector/Theme Strength:** Group is also trending higher
11. **Not [[extended stocks]]:** Within 5% of setup, not stretched above
12. **Market Alignment:** [[market trend model]] is positive

### The 7 Warning Signs to Avoid

From [[buying clues]], skip pullbacks with:

1. **Loose Price Action:** Erratic candles with long wicks—not strength, just noise
2. **No [[volume]] Support:** Breakouts without 1.5-2x average volume are fakeouts
3. **Below Key EMAs:** Price under EMA 20 or EMA 10—too early
4. **Failed Breakout History:** Two failed breakouts = dealbreaker
5. **No Sector Tailwind:** Weak sector = uphill battle
6. **No Compelling Story:** No [[institutional accumulation]], no EPS growth = pass
7. **Choppy Base:** Wide, deep consolidation shows indecision, not readiness

## Institutional Accumulation During Pullbacks

### 10 Footprints of Smart Money

From [[institutional accumulation]]:

1. **Repeated Up-Days on [[volume]]:** 1.5-2x average volume on strong closes
2. **Quiet Pullbacks:** Light red-day volume = weak selling pressure
3. **Tight Trading Ranges:** Narrow range after big move = absorption
4. **[[higher lows]]:** Staircase pattern of faster buybacks
5. **Strong [[relative strength]] Line:** New highs while market chops
6. **Support at Key MAs:** Respect for EMA 10 or EMA 20
7. **Volume Dry-Ups at Lows:** Collapsed volume = sellers done
8. **Cluster Breakouts in [[industry (previously known as sectors)]]:** Institutions buy themes, not individual stocks
9. **Earnings as Catalyst:** Intensified accumulation around earnings
10. **No Panic on Market Weakness:** Refuses to drop when indexes are red

## Five-Star Pullback Setup

### The 8-Step Framework

From [[five star setups]]:

1. **Prior Power Move:** Stock up 80%+ (ideally 100%+) in recent advance
2. **Clean, Straight Advance:** Strong, direct move—no choppy action
3. **Tight Consolidation:** Daily ranges shrink, volatility contracts, candles tighten
4. **Visible Accumulation:** Strong volume on up-days, quiet pullbacks
5. **Momentum Context:** Risk-on environment, leadership behavior, [[market trend model]]
6. **Real Catalyst:** Sales +20% YoY, EPS +50% YoY, or structural story
7. **Obvious Breakout Level:** Clear, well-tested resistance
8. **Tight Risk Management:** Precise entry, tight stop below base—[[assymetric bets]]

> "These setups don't come often—but when they come, they push your equity curve to new highs. Learn to wait. That's where the real money is." - From [[five star setups]]

## Pre-Trade Checklist for Pullbacks

### 7 Critical Checks Before Buying

From [[before opening a trade]]:

1. **Clean [[tight base]]:** Low volatility, tight stop below 10% (ideally <5%)
2. **Clear Story:** Sales growth 20%+, EPS growth 50%+, or real catalyst
3. **Liquidity:** >$200M daily dollar volume turnover (Price × Volume)
4. **[[volume]] Behavior:** Consistent volume, clear spikes on up days
5. **Defined Entry:** [[big base breakout]], tight consolidation, or controlled pullback
6. **[[assymetric bets]]:** Can this realistically move 50-100%?
7. **Price Action Quality:** [[higher lows]], tight bars, [[inside day]], compression before expansion

### 8 Rules Before Hitting Buy

1. **Only in Uptrends:** EMA10 > EMA20, price trades above them
2. **Volume Confirms:** 40%+ average volume on setup adherence/reclaim
3. **Never Chase Extensions:** Skip stocks >4x ATR% from 50MA
4. **Strong Themes Only:** AI, drones, batteries—capital inflows behind setup
5. **Clean Structure:** Flags, flat bases, VCP, cup-and-handle
6. **High [[relative strength]]:** Already outperforming market
7. **Solid Liquidity:** $200M+ average daily turnover, price >$10
8. **Entry Only on Strength:** Breakout or gap up—never randomly

## Psychology: Avoiding FOMO on Pullbacks

### How to Handle Extended Leaders

From [[extended stocks]]:

> "Most of the strongest stocks will often be extended when you notice them. Chasing leads to late entries and frustration."

**Discipline Framework:**

1. **Chart Distance:** If >5% above buy point or EMA 20, it's too extended
2. **[[ATR%]] Check:** Trading above 1x or more its ADR% means high risk
3. **Wait for [[tight base]]:** Every leader pauses—flags, VCPs reset risk/reward
4. **Avoid "Hope Entries":** Buying after +30% run is gambling, not trading
5. **Track Sector Rotation:** New leaders form in same [[industry (previously known as sectors)]]—prepare for next
6. **Learn from Past Winners:** TSLA, NVDA, CELH all had pauses where pros entered

> "Strong Stocks Give Second Chances: Wait for tight consolidations, pullbacks to the 20EMA, or a proper volatility compression pattern. That's where risk/reward resets." - From [[entering stocks late]]

## Risk Management

### Position Sizing

From [[high tight flag]]:
- [[position size]] to risk 0.15-0.3% of account
- Tight [[stop loss]] below 50 MA (~5% risk) or based on ATR% division

### Exit Strategy

From [[high tight flag]]:
- **[[sell into strength]]:** Scale out 1/3 to 1/2 after 3-5 days
- Move stop to breakeven
- Trail remainder with 10-day or 20-day MA (start with 10-day for beginners)
- Don't [[add position]]—treat re-flags as new trades
- Skip if [[extended stocks]] or narrative unchanged
