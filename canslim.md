CANSLIM Methodology: Prescriptive Instruction Set

The CANSLIM methodology supplies a seven-factor instruction set for filtering and selecting growth stocks. Apply the factors as a combined screen and qualitative review. A candidate must demonstrate strength across the quantitative earnings criteria (C and A) before advancing to the supporting factors (N, S, L, I). Execute purchases only when the market factor (M) confirms a favorable environment. Re-evaluate the full set after every quarterly earnings release, material news event, or significant shift in price/volume behavior. Stocks failing multiple factors receive no further consideration.

C: Current Quarterly Earnings Per Share Growth

Core prescriptive rule: Compute year-over-year EPS growth for the most recent completed quarter and require at least 25% positive growth, with preference for acceleration across recent quarters and corroboration from revenue growth.

Step-by-step application:

1. Obtain diluted EPS (or basic EPS if diluted unavailable) from the company’s latest quarterly report and the identical quarter one year earlier. Use operating or adjusted EPS figures that exclude one-time items when available.
2. Apply the growth formula: [ \text{EPS Growth (%)} = \frac{\text{EPS}{\text{most recent quarter}} - \text{EPS}{\text{same quarter prior year}}}{|\text{EPS}_{\text{same quarter prior year}}|} \times 100 ]
3. Require the result ≥ 25%. Values of 50% or higher strengthen the signal; values between 18–25% meet a weaker minimum cited in some implementations but fall short of the primary standard.
4. Compare growth rates for the prior two to four quarters. Accelerating or sustained high-single-quarter growth passes; material deceleration triggers rejection or downgrade.
5. Repeat the identical YoY calculation for revenue or net sales in the same quarters. Demand positive and preferably strong sales growth to confirm that EPS expansion reflects operational performance rather than cost cuts, share-count reduction, or non-recurring items.
6. Inspect the earnings release footnotes, management commentary, and any reconciliation tables for one-time gains, accounting changes, or seasonality distortions. Adjust or discard the growth figure if it cannot be substantiated as recurring.

Verification and red flags: Cross-check against the trailing twelve months (TTM) trend and sequential quarter comparisons. Reject cases where EPS growth appears solely from aggressive share repurchases without revenue or margin expansion, or where margins compress despite the headline EPS increase. Confidence level on the 25% primary threshold: high (consistent across standard implementations).

A: Annual Earnings Growth

Core prescriptive rule: Establish a track record of consistent annual EPS expansion over multiple years and require average or compound annual growth of at least 25% over the most recent three-to-five-year period.

Step-by-step application:

1. Collect annual diluted EPS figures for the last five fiscal years (or the longest available consistent series) plus the most recent TTM or current-year estimate if the fiscal year has not closed.
2. Compute year-over-year annual growth for each of the last three to five years. Then calculate the compound annual growth rate (CAGR) across the full multi-year window: [ \text{CAGR} = \left( \frac{\text{EPS}{\text{most recent}}}{\text{EPS}{\text{earliest in window}}} \right)^{1/n} - 1 ] where (n) equals the number of years in the window.
3. Require the multi-year CAGR or average annual growth rate ≥ 25%. Individual years may vary, but the overall trajectory must remain positive and robust.
4. Verify that EPS remained positive in each of the last three years and that growth occurred through varied economic conditions where possible.
5. Cross-reference with annual revenue growth and operating margin trends over the same horizon to confirm earnings expansion rests on scalable operations rather than financial engineering.

Verification and red flags: Examine 10-K filings and auditor notes for changes in accounting policies, extraordinary items, or discontinued operations that inflate prior-year comparisons. Reject candidates showing erratic annual results (sharp gains followed by sharp reversals) or growth concentrated in a single anomalous year. Confidence level on the 25% multi-year threshold and three-to-five-year window: high.

N: New Products, Services, Management, or Catalysts

Core prescriptive rule: Confirm the presence of material new developments—new products, services, management, industry conditions, or positive events—that are driving or capable of driving the stock to new price highs.

Step-by-step application:

1. Review the most recent 6–12 months of company press releases, earnings call transcripts, regulatory filings, and credible news sources for announcements of new products, services, patents, platform launches, major contracts, or management additions with relevant track records.
2. Assess materiality: the development must be capable of meaningfully affecting future revenue or margins within the next 1–3 years. Vague or incremental updates do not qualify.
3. Observe price and volume reaction: the stock must demonstrate follow-through by advancing to or near new 52-week highs, or by breaking out of a prior consolidation range on expanded volume after the catalyst becomes public.
4. Check industry context: determine whether the development positions the company as a beneficiary of secular shifts (technological, regulatory, or competitive) rather than a one-off event.

Verification and red flags: Discard purely promotional announcements lacking substantive detail or third-party validation. Ignore catalysts already fully priced in with no remaining upside surprise. Require the price action to confirm the fundamental development; absence of new highs after positive news downgrades the factor.

S: Supply and Demand

Core prescriptive rule: Establish evidence of constrained supply combined with rising demand, primarily through price action accompanied by above-average volume and, where present, share reduction mechanisms.

Step-by-step application:

1. Retrieve current shares outstanding (basic and fully diluted) and float from the latest balance sheet or ownership filings. Note any authorized or completed buyback programs and their execution progress.
2. Analyze daily or weekly price and volume charts over the prior 3–12 months. Require that significant price advances occur on volume at least 50–100% above the 20- or 50-day average volume.
3. Calculate or observe the up/down volume ratio over recent weeks or months: cumulative volume on up days should exceed volume on down days.
4. Assess float characteristics: smaller floats amplify moves when demand rises but increase volatility; treat very large floats as requiring correspondingly stronger volume confirmation.
5. Monitor for decreasing shares outstanding via buybacks as a secondary supply-constriction signal.

Verification and red flags: Reject rising prices on declining or below-average volume (weak demand). High-volume price declines signal distribution and override positive supply signals. Extremely low float stocks require extra scrutiny for liquidity risk and potential manipulation.

L: Leader or Laggard (Relative Strength)

Core prescriptive rule: Select stocks demonstrating leadership through superior relative price performance against the broad market and peers; require high relative strength ratings and an advancing relative strength line.

Step-by-step application:

1. Obtain or compute the relative strength (RS) rating: the percentile ranking of the stock’s price performance versus all other stocks over the past 12 months (or a comparable custom period). Require an RS rating of at least 80 (outperforming 80% of stocks); 90+ is stronger.
2. Plot or inspect the RS line (stock price divided by a benchmark index such as the S&P 500 or Nasdaq Composite). The RS line must trend upward and preferably make new highs in concert with or ahead of the stock price.
3. Compare the stock’s performance and RS ranking against its industry group and sector peers. The stock should rank among the leaders within its group.
4. Review shorter- and longer-term timeframes for alignment: strong RS on both weekly/monthly and daily charts increases conviction.

Verification and red flags: Stocks with RS ratings below 70 generally fail this factor. True laggards (persistently low RS) receive no credit even within strong groups unless accompanied by a clear, accelerating turnaround with supporting C and A factors. Confidence level on the ≥80 RS threshold: high.

I: Institutional Sponsorship

Core prescriptive rule: Verify meaningful and preferably increasing institutional ownership by quality sponsors, while avoiding excessive saturation that leaves limited room for further accumulation.

Step-by-step application:

1. Extract institutional ownership percentage and the number of institutional holders from the most recent 13F filings or aggregated data services. Target ownership in a moderate range (typically 30–70%) rather than near-zero or near-total saturation.
2. Compare the latest quarter’s institutional holdings against the prior quarter. Require a net increase in shares held or in the number of institutions.
3. Note the identity and track record of key institutional holders where disclosed; sponsorship by historically successful growth-oriented institutions adds weight.
4. Observe volume patterns for signs of institutional accumulation: large block trades or sustained volume spikes without corresponding retail-driven news flow.

Verification and red flags: Declining institutional ownership or heavy selling by multiple large holders overrides other positive factors. Extremely high ownership (>85–90%) signals the factor is exhausted. Low or zero institutional sponsorship fails the criterion outright.

M: Market Direction

Core prescriptive rule: Initiate or add to positions only when the broad market indexes trade in a confirmed uptrend; suspend new buying or adopt defensive positioning when the market enters a downtrend or heavy distribution phase.

Step-by-step application:

1. Daily or weekly, examine major indexes (S&P 500, Nasdaq Composite, Dow Jones Industrial Average, and relevant small-cap or sector benchmarks). Confirm the primary trend by checking price position relative to the 200-day simple moving average (SMA) and the slope of that average.
2. Require the index to trade above a rising 200-day SMA. Additional confirmation includes the 50-day SMA above the 200-day SMA and a pattern of higher highs and higher lows.
3. Count and monitor distribution days: high-volume down days that occur during an attempted advance. An accumulation of distribution days (typically three or more in quick succession) signals potential trend reversal.
4. Assess market breadth (advancers versus decliners, new highs versus new lows) and internal indicators to corroborate the price trend.
5. Rule application: When M is negative or transitioning to negative, do not enter new long positions regardless of how strongly individual stocks score on C–I. Existing positions require heightened monitoring and readiness to reduce exposure.

Verification and red flags: A stock satisfying C–I but purchased into a confirmed bear market or sharp correction will likely underperform or produce losses even if its fundamentals remain intact. Reassess M after every significant market move or at least weekly.

Overall application and integration instructions: Run quantitative screens first on C and A using earnings databases and financial platforms. Manually or programmatically layer N, S, L, and I reviews on the surviving list. Execute only under positive M conditions, ideally on technical breakouts confirming the N catalyst. Maintain a documented watchlist with scores or pass/fail status for each factor. After any earnings release or material event, re-score the affected factors immediately. The methodology functions as a selection filter; separate risk-management rules (position sizing, stop placement, portfolio exposure limits) operate outside this instruction set but remain mandatory for implementation.

This document contains the complete, self-contained prescriptive instruction set for the seven CANSLIM components. All thresholds and procedures reflect the most consistently cited standards across implementations. Minor variations exist in exact percentage minima (primarily 20–25% for earnings growth); the 25% level serves as the primary benchmark here.
